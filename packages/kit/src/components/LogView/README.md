# `LogView`

The log viewer: a find bar, a level quick filter, a windowed list of lines and a status strip over a `LogFeed` (design 16 S120). `docs/ui-next/optimisations.md` section 3 is the list of what it must not regress; the status files `P1b-2.md` (the part), `L1.md` (dropped lines) and `L2.md` (sources and order) say how it was built and measured.

## What order means

**The lines are in the order the feed gave them: arrival order.** `LogFeedLine.seq` numbers them from 1 as they came; the view shows them in that order and never sorts them.

With one source the arrival order and the order of the clocks are the same. With several sources they are not: the lines of a service's instances or an application's processes come interleaved, each source's lines are in its own clock order, and a line can carry a timestamp earlier than the line above it. The first real stream showed 110 of 2,225 rows like that. The clock column is what the source reported; the row's place is when the line reached the view.

The view does not sort because a live tail cannot: a sorted view would hold every line back until no earlier one can still arrive, and nothing says when that is. The command-line tools that follow several streams at once print them in arrival order as well. What follows from it:

- Anything that reads the clock as a position (a jump to a time, a range) treats it as approximate when the log has more than one source (the old viewer's `findEntryIndexByTime` already assumed arrival order, HLT-13).
- A line the view shows is never moved after it is drawn: a row, a selection, a match and the reader's place are named by the line's ordinal in the view, which does not change.

## Sources

A line may name its source (`LogFeedLine.source`, an id such as `orders-api/app`; `''` names none). A feed may also list the sources it follows (`LogFeedSnapshot.sources`: an id and, if it has one, a `label`). The view has **heard of** a source from either, whichever comes first.

- **One source (or none): nothing extra.** The rows are the clock, the level and the text; three column heads and `aria-colcount` 3.
- **More than one: a Source column**, between the level and the text, 168 px wide as the mock's is (`shell-panel.css` 103). Each row says its source in a chip: the name as text, cut from its start when it does not fit so that its end, which tells it from the others, stays (`MiddleEllipsis`, the kit's tooltip rule: the whole name is the tip while it is cut), and an 8 px swatch in the hue of the source's place in a series of seven (blue, green, amber, purple, cyan, red, gray: the mock's `SRC_COLORS`; the eighth source takes blue again). The swatch is never what says which source it is: the name is text. A line that names no source has an empty cell.
- **The column appears in the one commit that carries the second source** (a line, or the feed's listing) and **never goes away**, even when the lines of the second source have left the buffer, so the shape of the log does not change under a reader. Rows are a fixed height, so nothing moves vertically: no scroll command is issued for it, the window is the same, and no geometry is measured again.
- **The name** of a source is the `label` the feed's latest listing gives it, else its id: a label that goes away is the id again (the host's card names sources from the same listing, so the rows and the card say the same), a label that arrives later changes the tag of that source only, and a source the feed no longer lists keeps its last name (its lines are still in the log). A source keeps its hue for the life of the view.
- **The tip of a cut name** is the kit's (`TooltipHost`): the whole name, after the delay, while the name is cut. A tip describes the trigger the pointer is on and ends when that trigger moves (L3): a scroll that leaves it where it was does not end it. A list that follows a live stream moves its rows under a resting pointer, so a row's tip opens only when the row holds still for the delay (a stream slower than about two lines a second, a paused list, a list scrolled back): the reader of a fast stream pauses it or scrolls back, which stops the movement.
- **Search matches the text of a line only**, as the mock's does (`shell-panel.js` 1668 and 1953 test the message): a query that is only in a source's name finds nothing.
- **Copy carries the source of every line that has one**, whether or not the column shows (L3): `clock LEVEL label  text`, the level padded to five and two spaces after the label, as the mock's `lineText` copies a line (`shell-panel.js` 2062). A line that names no source is copied as `clock LEVEL text`.

## The clock

The clock of a row is `HH:MM:SS.mmm` in the viewer's local time, the mock's default (`shell-panel.js` 1572 `ts: 'local'`). The `timestamps` prop is the mock's Timestamps option: `'utc'` writes `HH:MM:SS.mmmZ` (the Z says which clock it is, `shell-panel.js` 1708) in a 120 px column instead of 112 px (`shell-panel.css` 92 and 93). It is a setting of the view: changing it draws the mounted rows again and keeps every line. The control that offers the choice is the toolbar's (P1b-4); the mock's third choice, Hidden, comes with it. A copy carries the clock the rows show. The tip of the clock is the line's own timestamp (the source's text with its date, zone and every digit of its fraction: an RFC 3339 timestamp with nanoseconds, as a command-line log tool prints it), so a local clock can be read as UTC and the date it leaves out is there.

## The controls

The mock's Logs tool has a vertical toolbar beside the view (`shell-regions.js` 298-320): the toolbar is the host's, and the view gives it hands. `createLogViewControls()` makes an object that the toolbar and the view share: give it to the view (`<LogView controls={controls} />`) and read it as a store (`useSyncExternalStore(controls.subscribe, controls.getSnapshot)`).

- **It says five facts, and says them rarely**: whether the view follows the end (`following`), whether the list draws a line (`drawn`) and whether the view holds one, whatever the level filter shows (`held`), the feed's own pause (`paused`) and where the feed is (`feed`). A line that arrives changes none of them after the first, so a toolbar is not drawn again for a line (the model tells them from the frame that published a change and the moment the feed says it paused, resumed, ended or failed, so a control and the strip, which read the feed's status, are drawn in one commit).
- **`follow(on)`** is the strip's Jump to latest (`true`: the end of the log, following, and a paused feed is let through, as the mock's Follow does) or stops following where the list is (`false`).
- **`pause(on)`** holds the feed's reads or lets them through: the pause is the feed's, which goes on holding the lines it is sent (bounded, the oldest dropped and counted), and the strip says how many wait (the mock's `Paused, N new lines waiting`). The view draws none of them until it is lifted.
- **`clear()`** forgets everything made so far, as the mock's Clear does (`logClear`, shell-panel.js 2083-2090: its buffer, view and queue are emptied, its dropped count is 0, its search restarts). What the view holds, what it had evicted and the marker of lines the feed dropped before it read them are forgotten and not told as dropped (L1's rule that every line is a line shown or a line told dropped holds from the clear on); what the feed drops or the view evicts from now is told as it comes. What the feed holds that the view had not read was made before the clear too (the lines that came while it was paused, the lines of the last frame, and a drop it had not yet told): it is read and thrown away, so only the lines made after the clear are shown. A feed that is paused stays paused; the level filter and the query stay; the list goes to the end while it follows, to its top when it did not. The mock also writes a marker row, `Cleared. New lines continue below`, which the view does not.
- **`copy(timestamps)`** is what a Copy control writes: every line the list draws, as the mock's toolbar Copy writes the lines of its view (`shell-panel.js` 2063-2070; the level filter applies, a marker is written as its own sentence and is no line), a line each, the source of each included and the clock `timestamps` names: the text the keyboard's copy writes. A selection does not narrow it: the selected lines are the keyboard's copy (the list's `copy` event; the mock's keyboard copy is the active line, 3091-3096). Undefined when the list draws no line. The kit reaches no clipboard: the caller writes the text.
- Until a view is bound, and once it has gone (a view hidden by React's `Activity` releases its effects and lets go), the object says what it said last and does nothing when asked.

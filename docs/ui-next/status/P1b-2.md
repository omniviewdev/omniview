## Decision rule and budgets (written 2026-11-19, BEFORE any measurement; measured since: docs/ui-next/baseline/2026-11-19-log.md, applied exactly as written)

The bench is `bench/log/` (written after this rule): a seeded synthetic source into a 100,000-line buffer at 1,000, 10,000 and 50,000 lines a second for 10 s each, in WebKit and Chromium, with the control being the same page with a plain-div list over the same window maths; the 1-minute load average before and after each run, a run above 12 repeated.

- Follow tail, rAF interval p95: at most 16.7 ms at 10,000 lines/s in Chromium; in WebKit at most the control's p95 plus 10%. At 50,000 lines/s no budget: reported, with the longest frame.
- React commits per second: at most 60, at every rate (bounded by the frame rate, not the line rate). Exact count in CI (jsdom, exists): 1 commit per frame however many batches.
- DOM rows: at most the viewport plus overscan and the step's slack (48 at 1440 x 900), at every rate and after 100,000 lines; exact in CI (exists).
- Memory at the cap: the heap after 100,000 lines held is within 2 x the old viewer's measured 24 MB per 100,000 lines of the stream's buffer (P1b-1), flat after another 1,000,000 lines go by.
- Search over 100,000 lines: a literal and a simple regex finish within 250 ms in total, and no slice holds a frame longer than 8 ms.
- Scroll through 100,000 lines (30 pages): rAF interval p95 at most 16.7 ms in Chromium, relative to the control in WebKit.
- Overscan: 10 against 30 on the scroll workload; the smaller wins unless the larger is faster by more than the noise.
- A budget missed is reported as missed, not moved.

## Decisions and deviations for the lead

- **Cap 100,000** (the inventory, MNR-L2, P1b-1) against the mock's 10,000 (S120 and `LOG_CAP`, its simulation cap). The strip uses the mock's words for what is dropped.
- **Fonts**: the message is JetBrains Mono; the time and the level are the UI face with tabular numerals, as the mock's cells are (the assignment says "monospace rows"; the mock is the reference).
- **No level**: a muted dash (`MissingValue`, the mock's `H.none('No level')`); the level filter counts such a line as info (the mock's `?? 1`).
- **The level filter** is private to the part (a radio group with the mock's `.seg` paint); it can become a shared part when a second screen needs it.
- **Highlights** are spans in the row, not the CSS Custom Highlight API: the registry of highlights is document-wide, and two views mounted at once (hidden panel sessions) would take each other's.
- **`defaultQuery`** exists for the examples and tests.
- **Follow** is the view's own state; P1b-3's Follow toggle needs a controlled prop (not added: no screen uses it yet).
- A source's drop (`LogFeedBatch.dropped`) is a marker row in its place; the view's own eviction is only counted in the strip.
- **The match cap bounds what is held** (review finding on the search, answered by the lead's instruction to choose): a search the match cap stopped resumes after eviction. The old viewer re-scanned everything on an eviction (`evictionCount`), so a capped search there recovered too, at the cost of a full scan per eviction.
- **The regex guard errs towards refusing** (a static check, a time check behind it that runs between lines): safe patterns with a repeated group that holds an unbounded repeat read `Invalid pattern`; documented in the file's header and above. A parser-based or bounded-time matcher would be the way to remove the false positives and the two uncovered classes.
- **Search words**: `Search stopped` for a stopped search that holds nothing (the mock has no such state).
- **Budget readings** (analysis header, written before the matrix ran): a time or rate budget is judged on the median over the 10 rounds with [min-max]; "no frame over 8 ms" on every frame of every round; exact counts strictly; 60 commits a second as `60.0` at one decimal. The kit at an overscan of 30 is B7's experiment and not counted in B3.
- **Four behaviours changed in review round 4, for the lead to keep or reverse.** (1) *A row's marks are scanned under the search's clock, not by a count of passes.* A row used to stop after 10,000 passes (empty matches included), so a line the search had found after more empty matches than that was not marked; it now reads the clock every 4,096 empty matches against the search's own budget (`SEARCH_TIME_BUDGET_MS`, 100 ms) and ends past it, as the search does, so a line the search found is marked. The cost is that a pathological line (a pattern that matches the empty string at every position of a line of millions of characters) can hold the render of its row for up to that budget, where it held it for about a millisecond: only a row the search found a match in is scanned, so a line the search itself gave up on is never drawn with marks. (2) *The strip reads the feed's own pause flag.* `LogFeedSnapshot.paused` is true "whatever `state` says", and the strip took the pause from `state === 'paused'`: a feed paused while it starts (state `starting`) or one that goes on to stream while paused showed no Resume. The runtime's stream maps a pause to state `paused` itself (`logStream.ts:327`), so it could not show this; the contract and the synthetic feed can. The strip now offers Resume to a feed that is paused in any state that is not over. (3) *A failure that is not a failed creation says the stream stopped.* "Couldn't start the log stream" is for stage `create`; a `session` or `source` failure reads "The log stream stopped", with or without lines (it read "Couldn't start" for a stream that had streamed no line). The feed does not say in which phase a `session` failure came, so one that comes before the stream's first line also reads "stopped". (4) *A selection of lines lets go of a text selection.* Select all, and a range made with Shift (the pointer or the keys), clear the browser's text range, which the copy stands aside for; a press without Shift and the plain arrows leave it as it was, so a drag over a row's words still selects.

The page speaks version 2 of the stream and deletes what it guessed in version 1: there is one pathway for each fact. Worktree `~/omniview/wt/t3cp-v2`, branch `wip/t3cp-v2` (`ui-next/t3-channel-page` with `feat/terminal-channel-released-and-applied` merged, `7acb2870`). Not pushed.

What the page does now:

- `attach.v` is 2 (`CHANNEL_VERSION`, `channel.ts`). `terminalChannel.ts` decodes `0x87 applied` (a u64 that is a safe integer, exactly 9 bytes, else the stream is closed as for any frame it cannot read), `0x88 released` and `0x89 unsupported {"v":n}`. `ChannelEvents` has `applied`, `released`, `unsupported`; `takenOver` is no longer an end of the stream.
- Input: the feed keeps every byte it typed after the last count the host gave (`ready.input_applied`, then each `applied`), in pieces of at most 64 KiB, merged while they wait. After the `ready` of a stream that was connected again it sends exactly the bytes after `input_applied`, from the first, before anything typed since, and each `applied` trims. No byte is typed twice and none is lost. The bytes kept are bounded by `MAX_KEPT_INPUT_BYTES` (1 MiB, the host's queue): a page that types more than the host has applied fails in words (`More was typed than the terminal has taken ...`). A `ready` without `input_applied` is unreadable (the feed fails in words); a number above what the page typed is taken as all of it, with one warning. The first `ready` sets the base: what an earlier page typed (a reload) is not this page's.
- `taken-over` keeps the stream open as a watcher: the feed is `ended` with `detached`, input off, nothing sent on it. `released` sets `detached.free` at once, with no question and no timer. `ended` to a watcher gives the exit and leaves `detached` without `free`, and the end is not this page's to dismiss (`fate` stays `not-ours`). A watcher the host closes with no word (it watched longest of five, or the connection dropped) cannot be told when the terminal is free: the page asks the host (`refresh`), and asks again on the schedule of a reconnect (250 ms, 1 s, 4 s) only while the host cannot be reached for the question; an answer is final.
- `unsupported` fails in words that name the version the host speaks (or say another version when it names none); nothing connects again.

Deleted: `LOST_REFRESH_MS` and the 5 s interval with its focus and visibility listeners (`terminals.ts`), `refreshLost` and `hasLost` and the registry's `isLost` (`TerminalSessions.ts`), the `waiting` string and the dead-socket re-queue in `pump` (a link that refuses a piece leaves it kept; the resend after `ready` covers it, so there is one pathway), the `takenOver` end of the stream in `terminalChannel.ts`. ## Closing a tab: the close travels on the stream (protocol v2, `0x06 close`)

A feed that holds a live stream after `ready` ends its terminal with `close` (0x06) on that stream and waits for the stream's answer: `ended` (the host ended it, the stream was the holder), `close-refused` (0x8a: the stream had been displaced without the page knowing, nothing ended, the other tab keeps its terminal), or the stream's end. The decision that the stream holds the terminal and the decision that it ends are one step in the host, so a displaced tab that closes cannot end what another tab shows. A feed that made its terminal and never opened a stream still uses `CloseChannelTerminal` (nobody else can hold it).

What remains of the earlier cost, stated plainly: a tab closed while its stream is down (dropped, being connected again, failed by `CONNECTION_LOST` or `OUTPUT_GAP`, or not yet `ready`) sends no close and ends nothing; the terminal lives until the host's lease ends it (5 minutes by default, 30 at most, then `closed_by: detach-timeout`). The protocol has no stream that asks without taking the terminal (an attach takes it, and a `close` as the first frame is not an attach: the one `not-found`), so the page cannot become a holder just to close, and `CloseChannelTerminal` would end what another tab may hold. If the stream drops while a close is in flight, the same holds: the lease ends it.

## One presenter (the lead's ruling of 2026-11-21)

- **While the centre is open the toast stack is suspended** (`hold('center')`: nothing is drawn, no clock runs). **A notice that arrives or changes while it is open raises no toast**: it is in the list at once, it is told ONCE to a screen reader through the window's live regions (the model's `say`, as a record without a toast is, S86), and it costs ONE React commit (the list, the bell and the live region change together: `Centre.commits.test.tsx`; the probe counts it in a real engine). The model change is one condition in `core/notices/store.ts` `decide` (`holds.center` leaves no toast policy), the only edit to the model: a gap proved red by 4 tests (`status/O1b-2/red-model-presenter.txt`) and, in the whole window, by `PluginHost.centre.test.tsx` (`status/O1b-2/red-host-presenter.txt`).
- **The palette is a modal and the centre closes when it opens** (`plugins/host/notices.ts`, `attach`). What arrives while the palette is open waits and is drawn when it closes (the model's modal hold, unchanged).
- **O1b-1's hold, with the centre.** A toast that was already on screen when the centre opened waits with the stack and comes back, when the centre closes, with the time it had left (at least two seconds: `Centre.read.test.tsx`, "the toast waits and returns with its remaining time") UNLESS its record was read in the centre, whether by closing it after at least a second, by opening the row or by acting on it: then it does NOT come back ("is not brought back after a read of at least 1 s", "after a row is opened"). **Decided against the mock, on purpose:** the mock's `read()` only sets `read` (`shell-notify.js:622-628`) and its `toastEntries()` tests `toastDismissed` and `held` and not `read` (`:1154-1160`), so the mock would show the toast of a record the person has just read again when the row is gone. The model's rule (O1a: "the toast of a record that was removed or read is dropped", `lifetime.test.ts:311`) and the lead's instruction are kept: a message that was read in the list is not shown to the person a second time.
- **What it costs later:** a RUNNING operation that starts while the centre is open also raises no toast, and has none when the centre closes (the model decides at the start). Operations are out of scope here (the Active group, below), but whoever builds them must decide whether the toast is raised and held, as the mock's is, so that it comes back.

## Spec errata (for the errata pass, row N10; found here, not chosen silently)

1. **S89a and the mock's one-presenter rule disagree about a non-modal surface.** The chapter says "Non-modal surfaces do not suspend feedback" (`research/int-ui/16-ide-shell.md:445`, the last sentence of S89a), so a non-modal centre would leave the toast stack running over it. The mock says the opposite for a centre: "ONE PRESENTER. A toast is not raised, and a raised one is dropped, while a live inline rendering of the same record is visible …, while a centre or Operations row of that record is on screen" (`shell-notify.js:37`), and its code leaves a toast out while a row of its record is drawn (`presented()` at `shell-notify.js:1144`, `toastEntries()` at `:1154-1160`). **The lead chose one presenter** (2026-11-21) because the same notice drawn twice at once is noise: the centre suspends the stack and a notice that arrives while it is open raises no toast. The chapter's sentence holds for the palette (modal) and is wrong for the centre; the model's README (`core/notices/README.md`, "One presenter while the centre is open") records the rule.
2. **The chapter does not say what happens to a toast whose record is read in the centre; the mock's code keeps it.** S81 lists when a record is read; S89a says what a modal does to toasts; nothing says whether a toast outlives the read of its record. The mock's `read()` only sets `read` (`shell-notify.js:622-628`), `toastPolicy()` (`:1028-1034`) and `decide()` (`:1055-1070`) never look at it, and `toastEntries()` tests `toastDismissed` and `held` and not `read` (`:1154-1160`), so the toast of a record the person has just read in the centre comes back when the centre closes, as long as it still has time (a sticky failure always has). Built as the model's rule (O1a, `lifetime.test.ts:311`) and the lead's instruction have it: the toast of a read record is dropped and does not come back (above, "One presenter").

## Seam (scenario `q`, phase `centre`, `ui-next/plugins/e2e/seam.check.mjs`)

The lead's sequence "dismissed → in the centre, unread 1 → open → read" could not hold as written: **dismissing a toast marks its record read** (the model's `dismiss`; the mock, `shell-notify.js:631`; S81). The lead accepted the correction; the seam proves, in the real Go host in server mode, in WebKit and Chromium, from a window with nothing saved:

- **(a)** a failing page command is a toast; the person DISMISSES it; the bell's name is exactly `Notifications` and it has no dot; the centre opened from the bell lists the record (`Timed out`, the plugin's sentence) READ (no `data-unread`, no word Unread); the focus is on the row's title; Enter opens the row and it offers Retry (this window made it); Escape closes the centre and the focus is on the bell.
- **(b)** a second failure whose toast is NOT dismissed: the bell says `Notifications, 1 unread, 1 error`; opening the centre takes the toast out of the layer (one presenter) and the row is unread; a third failure that arrives while the centre is open is in the list, has no toast (a MutationObserver counts every toast the page draws: none) and is told ONCE in the window's live regions; after more than 1 s Escape closes the centre: the bell is `Notifications` again, no toast comes back, the focus is on the bell.
- **(c)** after the history has reached the window service's `notices` slot (the check polls `GetState`) and a RELOAD: the bell has no dot, the restore drew no toast and told nothing, the centre lists the three records, none unread, the restored `Timed out` row offers NO Retry and says `Retry is unavailable. Not available after restart.`

`forgetSession` now also writes `null` to the `notices` slot, because the history a scenario leaves would otherwise be restored into the next scenario's window. The phase writes no `pageerror` and no new 4xx/5xx (`RUNTIME_ALLOW` is unchanged).

**How each assertion was shown to bite** (CentreSeam, scratch copies, deleted; the served bundle patched with `page.route`, both engines): (a) dismiss no longer sets `read` → "a dismissed toast's record is read: the bell's name is exactly Notifications"; (b) the centre never calls `hold('center')` → "opening the centre takes the toast out of the layer (one presenter)"; (c) the page leaves, the slot is cleared, the page opens again → "the centre lists the three restored records". With the named line removed, in WebKit, a neighbouring assertion on the same fact tripped instead ("and the bell has no dot", "a notice that arrives while the centre is open has no toast", "one of them is the Timed out record"): no fault passed undetected, but the faults were not shown undetected with a whole group removed. NOT shown to bite: the live regions' emptiness after the restore, the restore's "no toast was drawn" count, and the "told ONCE" line (the unit tests prove those; the seam's line was added after the scratch runs and passed in both engines).

What the real host showed that the unit tests could not: the round trip works (the saver's 400 ms, the window service writing the slot, the restore on reload); and **clearing the slot with `SaveState` and then reloading does NOT make the history disappear**: the page's `pagehide` flush writes the history back from the store. ## The day grouping and the time zone (CI finding on #647)

**What CI showed.** `UI component tests` failed on `Centre.test.tsx > the list > lists the history newest first, under Recent and a heading for each day`: the third heading was `# Tue, Nov 14` where the test wanted `# Yesterday`. It passed on the author's machine (America/Chicago), in all 5,147 root tests there.

**Which it was: (a), a TEST that built its history from a fixed instant while the view groups by LOCAL day.** The test took `START` (1,700,000,000,000 = 2023-11-14 22:13:20 UTC) and a 26-hour span. In Chicago (UTC-6) that is the 14th at 16:13 to the 15th at 18:14: one local midnight, so "Yesterday". In UTC it is the 14th at 22:13 to the 16th at 00:14: TWO local midnights, so the oldest record is two local days back and its heading is a date. Every zone whose offset is between -0:13 and +1:46 fails it (UTC, Paris, Lagos, London in winter). Reproduced before the test was touched (`status/O1b-2/tz-before.txt`): `TZ=UTC` and `TZ=Europe/Paris` fail exactly that one test of the 5,150 of the root suite; `Pacific/Kiritimati` (+14), `Pacific/Pago_Pago` (-11), `Asia/Kolkata` (+5:30) and `America/Chicago` pass; the kit's own suite (2,340 tests) passes in all six.

**Was the product wrong too? Yes, in one place, and not the one CI showed.** The grouping itself is by the viewer's LOCAL date (`dayKey`, `dayLabel`) and was right. It is now proved against an independent oracle (`Intl` with the zone named, not the machine's) in 12 zones, hour by hour around every clock change of 2026 (days of 23, 25, 23 1/2 and 24 1/2 hours, and the clock changes AT midnight of Havana, Beirut and Cairo) and around the end of a month, of a year and of a leap February: `centreList.test.ts`, 132 tests. Three mutations (a day is always 24 hours; yesterday is 24 hours ago; the day key is the UTC date) each fail exactly the zones where they are wrong. **The defect:** the centre took "now" once, when it opened (`openedAt`), so a centre left open across midnight went on saying Today for yesterday's records and put every notice that arrived after midnight under a DATE heading ("Sun, Nov 15") instead of Today. **The mock's rule:** `dayLabel` reads the clock whenever the list is drawn (`now = new Date()`, `shell-feedback.js:657-660`), and the signature of what it drew has the hour in it (`Math.floor(Date.now() / 3600000)`, `:758`) so that a draw after the hour changed is not skipped; it has no timer for midnight (its only timer is the 10 s tick that rewrites the time labels, `shell-notify.js:1470-1474`), so its headings are right at the next draw and not before. **Built here:** a day's heading is the AGE of its day on the page's one age clock (`DayHeading`, `dayAge`, the clock that keeps the rows' times): Today turns into Yesterday at the local midnight by itself, the clock never sleeps more than a minute and reads again when the page is visible again, and a heading that appears later (the first notice of a new day) says what the day is now. `listOf` no longer takes `now`: it holds the day (`{ kind: 'day', day }`, the first instant of that local day) and not its words. Tests first, seen red: `red-days.txt` (12 roll-over tests failed, in 6 zones), `red-days-pure.txt` (61 of the new pure tests failed before the helpers existed); green after.

**What else was checked for the same assumption.** In `ui-next/shell/notices/**` and the model the only other local-calendar code is `clockOf` (the clock time a mute ends: local by design, its test builds the expectation with the same call); the toast layer has no date; the model's 7 days is a span of time. **The same test class elsewhere in the repo: none found.** A grep of the tests of `ui-next/**` and `packages/kit/**` for the local-time APIs and the words Today and Yesterday finds eight files: three of the kit (they pin a zone or build the expectation from the same getters), the panel card's and the centre's word test (local fields or the same call), `centreList.test.ts` (local fields), `Centre.test.tsx` (this one), and `persist.test.ts` (the word "yesterday" in prose). **Convention.** A test that depends on the local day takes its times from local fields (`new Date(2026, 10, 14, 12)`) and puts the process in the zones it is about (`vi.stubEnv('TZ', zone)`: V8 follows `process.env.TZ` as it is written, and the kit's tests already rely on it). `ZONES` in `notices/testing.tsx` is the list; `teardown` takes the stub away; `mountWindow({ at })` starts the model's clock and the page's `Date` at a chosen instant, where the default `START` is a UTC instant that is on different local days in different zones. **For the working rules (the lead's file):** CI is UTC and the maintainers' machines are not; run the root suite once under `TZ=UTC` and once under a far zone (`TZ=Pacific/Kiritimati`) before pushing a change that groups, labels or formats by day.

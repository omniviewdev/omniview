**Where it goes: three things, and two of them are not scenarios.**

1. **An 8 s timer that nothing cleared.** Every wait for a host to exit was `Promise.race([exited, new Promise((resolve) => setTimeout(resolve, 8000))])`; the race resolves when the host exits and the timer stays, so the Node process, finished with its work, lived 8 s more. Found by time stamping the output of a control that does 0.2 s of work: it printed its verdict 1.2 s after it started and the process ended 9.3 s after (`time`, and the stamps in the log of the second run). Each of the 27 control runs of each engine paid it: **216 s of the 560 the controls took**, in each engine. Now `untilExited` clears the timer: that control takes 1.2 s locally (it was 9.3 s); the runner's numbers are in the pull request.
2. **One job for everything.** The seam (10 minutes) and the controls (10 minutes) and the setup (3) run one after the other in a job for each engine. Nothing in them depends on another: each phase starts from a window with nothing saved on a host it opens pages on, each control runs one scenario on a host of its own.
3. Not changed here (below): the host build, 105 s, which every job repeats, and the fixture builds of 7 to 9 s in each run of a control that needs the first host.

The proxy failure the lead saw (`proxy.golang.org`, a run that failed twice) is in the host build; a shard is more of it (6 builds in a run, not 2), and a build job that makes the binary once is the answer to that and to the repeated 105 s (section 5).

## 2. What it is now

- **The check selects.** `PHASE_IDS` in `seam.check.mjs` is the list of its 26 phases, in order, and the full run is a loop over it (the 26 `await phase(...)` lines are one table of functions whose keys the check asserts equal `PHASE_IDS`). `SEAM_PHASES=<id>,<id>` runs only those, in order, on a host of its own; an id that is not in the list is an error; it is not used with a control or a `*_REPEAT`. A phase that passes prints `SEAM-PHASE <engine> <id> <seconds>`. `SEAM_LIST=1` prints the phases and the control runs as JSON and runs nothing. A run with none of these is the run it was.
- **The table.** `scripts/ci/seam-units.mjs`: 16 units, each with its `seconds` on the runner: eight of phases (`pages`, `settings`, `terminal-core`, `terminal-restart`, `terminal-find`, `session`, `plugin-process`, `connections-problems`), eight of control runs. A control run is `SEAM_CONTROL=<run>`: one control for the plain ones, a whole table for the five scenarios that have one (`process`, `failed-load`, `plugins-page`, `connections`, `problems-operations`).
- **The plan.** `seam-shards.mjs plan` packs the units (`probe-pack.mjs`) into the fewest shards of at most 420 s of work (the start of the host of a shard's phases, 15 s, is added), and writes the matrix: every shard, in each engine. **26 phases, 104 controls, 1,100 s listed: 3 shards of 380 to 385 s, 6 jobs.** A shard is planned at 420 s and a job at about 175 s of setup plus that: 9.9 minutes; the job's timeout is 20 minutes (the slowest setup, 260 s, and the ceiling of a shard, 546 s, are 13.5 minutes).
- **The report.** Each shard, after its steps and whatever they did, reads their logs (`SEAM-PHASE` lines from the seam step; the `detected` lines and the seconds each control run left), holds each unit to its seconds (past the seconds plus the larger of a quarter and 60 s it fails, with the number to put in the file, as rule 25 does for the probes), holds the shard to 546 s (the 420 s limit and the 1.3 of the slowest machine the kit probes' record has seen), and writes `seam-ran-<engine>-<shard>.json`, which it uploads.
- **The verdict.** `Seam check` runs whatever happened (`always()`): it requires the plan and every shard to have succeeded (and names the failed job and the scenario and assertion from its annotation, as before), then downloads every `seam-ran-*` and runs `seam-shards.mjs verify`: **both engines, every shard of the plan, every unit once and passed, and every phase and every control of the check, by the check's own list, seen once**. A scenario that is in the check and in no unit is red in the plan job (`check`) and again in the verdict; a shard that failed, timed out, never reported or ran no scenario is red; a plan of no shard is an error.
- **Nothing is selected.** Every phase and every control runs on every run, in WebKit and Chromium. The two controls the old step never ran are now run (section 4).

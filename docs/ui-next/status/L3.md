## (1) L2-03: a tip in a list that follows a live stream

**What the mock does** (read, not run: I did not run its tooltip against its live log): the same rule as the kit's. `core.js:3529-3535` `scroll(ev)`: `moves(tip.owner) || moves(tip.pending)` then `hideTip()`, for any scroll of anything that holds the trigger (`core.js:3600` registers it on the document, capture). Its log pins on every flush, `shell-panel.js:1752` `pin` (`leaf.scrollTop = leaf.scrollHeight`), `:1841` in `flushLog`, fed by a ticker every 100 ms (`:1655`, `genTick` `:1626-1653`), so a trigger inside its following log has the same defect.

**What VS Code and JetBrains do** (sources read 2026-11-20): VS Code's workbench hover service (`src/vs/platform/hover/browser/hoverService.ts`, microsoft/vscode `main` ac356367) closes a hover on a mouse down outside (`:346`), a click on the target (`:355`), a key down (`:363-364`) and when the target stops intersecting the viewport (an `IntersectionObserver`, `:370-373`, `_intersectionChange` `:526-531`); it listens for no scroll or wheel. Its editor's content hover does close on a scroll (`contentHoverController.ts:112,129-134`), because it is anchored to text that scrolls. IntelliJ's `IdeTooltipManager.kt` (JetBrains/intellij-community `master` 2a1ff23c) `eventDispatched` (`:320-400`) handles mouse entered, exited, moved, pressed and dragged and has no wheel case: a scroll does not hide a tip, and the next mouse move asks the component under the pointer again (`:362-386`).

**What the engines do for a pointer at rest under a scroll** (measured, `L3/boundary.mjs.txt`, steps of 24 px every 50 ms, then quiet): Chromium sends `pointerout` and `pointerover` for the new element after EVERY step, just before the `scroll` event; WebKit sends nothing while the scrolling goes on and one `pointerout` + `pointerover` + `pointermove` about 100 ms after the last step.

**The rule (decided here, state it to the lead):** a tip describes the trigger the pointer is on, so a scroll ends it when that trigger is no longer where the pointer reached it: its rectangle changed by more than a pixel (a trigger scrolled out of the window is one that moved). A scroll that leaves the trigger where it was (a head that sticks, a fixed control) leaves the tip, and a waiting delay, alone. A tip that is about to open for a trigger that moved while it waited does not open. **A row that scrolls under a resting pointer is another trigger**: the old row's tip ends at the scroll (never a tip for a row that is gone), and the row now under the pointer gets its own tip after the usual 500 ms once the engine says the pointer is over it (`pointerover` after each step in Chromium, once the scrolling settles in WebKit): so in a list that follows a stream the tip of a row opens only when the row holds still for the delay (a stream slower than about two lines a second, a paused list, a list scrolled back). A reader of a fast stream pauses it or scrolls back, which stops the movement; no tip opens in a fast stream and none ever stands for a row that is gone. Cost: with no tip shown or waiting a scroll does nothing (asserted: 100 scrolls, no rectangle read, no frame asked for, no hit test); with one, a scroll of something that holds the trigger reads its rectangle once.

Built in `TooltipHost/controller.ts`: `reached` (the trigger's rectangle when the pointer reached it, or when it took focus), `moved()` (a pixel is rounding), `onScroll`, and `show()` refusing a trigger that moved while it waited. The mock's file keeps its rule; the kit's `TooltipHost` documents the change.

**Numbers** (real pointer, real host, both engines, 1440 x 900, machine load 80 to 113 from other sessions; `L3/live-tip.mjs.txt`, and the probe's `scroll` section): a tip on the sticky head of a list that follows 20 lines a second **opens** (18 to 19 scroll events before it opens, 41 to 43 while it stayed) in Chromium and WebKit; with the pointer on a row at 20 lines a second, 153 frames, **no frame had a tip open and none had a tip that was not the row under the pointer**; when the stream stops the row under the pointer gets its tip after 500 ms (Chromium) and 600 ms (WebKit); at a line every 1.4 s six different rows got their own tip in 8.5 s, 0 frames with a tip for a row that was gone. L2's tool measured the head's tip never opening (37 scrolls in 1.8 s).

## (4) TIME ZONE

**The mock, log rows** (`shell-panel.js`): the viewer's local clock by default (`:1572` `ts: 'local'`; `:129-132` `clock(ms, { utc })` reads `getHours()` unless `utc`), UTC as a display option: every row draws a local span and a UTC span with a `Z` (`:1708`), the CSS shows the one the list's `data-ts` names (`shell-panel.css:108-110`; `:92-93` the Time column is 112 px, 120 px in UTC); the option is in Display options (`:2666`, Timestamps: Local time, UTC, Hidden). The line inspector (a double click or Enter, `:3057`, `:3114`) shows `Time (local)` (`stamp`: `toLocaleString`, `:133`) and `Time (UTC)` (`toISOString`, `:2099`); the JSON export has `time: toISOString()` (`:2075`). **No tip on a row's time**, and the text copy writes the LOCAL clock whatever the option says (`lineText` takes `clock(l.t)`, `:2062`). **The mock's pages**: every absolute time is UTC to the minute, in a tip: `k8s-resource-definitions.js:154` `stampOf`; an age's long form `<stamp> · <ago>` (`:217`); `Created` (`:910`, a `timestamp` field) is the compact relative label with the raw RFC 3339 text as its tip (`k8s-resources.js:234-237` `compactTime`, `:345`); Started and Last exit tips (`:1463`, `:1474`). So the mock itself draws log clocks local and page times UTC. **kubectl**: `kubectl logs --timestamps` prints RFC 3339 with nanoseconds, UTC (`2026-11-20T03:22:38.782123456Z`; the kubelet writes `RFC3339NanoFixed`, `2006-01-02T15:04:05.000000000Z07:00`, kubernetes/kubernetes#91186; the wire of P1b-3b carried `Z` timestamps). **The kit's helpers**: `formatClock(timestamp, { utc })` (`log/time.ts`, local or UTC, no mark) and the table's `ageTip` (`table/age/formatAge.ts:61`, UTC always, `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM UTC · 2 days ago`).

**Built:** `LogView` takes `timestamps?: 'local' | 'utc'` (default `'local'`, the mock's; type `LogTimestamps`, in the API report). `'utc'` writes `HH:MM:SS.mmmZ` and the Time column is 120 px (`.root[data-ov-timestamps='utc']`); a change draws the mounted rows again and keeps every line (the same elements). The toggle is P1b-4's, as is the mock's third choice, Hidden. **The full timestamp with its zone** is the tip of a row's clock (`data-ov-tip`, the line's own RFC 3339 text: date, zone, every digit of the fraction): an attribute the one tooltip reads, no element and no handler more in a row. The mock has no such tip; it is the cheapest way (no formatting per row) to put the UTC instant and the date beside a local clock, and a reader can compare it with the page's UTC. **Copy** carries the clock the rows show: local without a mark, UTC with its Z. The mock copies local always (an oversight I take it, since its UTC rows would copy a clock they did not show): a decision for the lead.

**What the detail page (plugin repo, not changed) should do:** follow the mock: show a compact relative age (`8m`) for `Created` and keep the UTC stamp in its tip, as `k8s-resources.js:234-237` does with the raw timestamp and `:154`/`:217` with `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM UTC · 8 minutes ago`; the plugin prints the stamp as the row's text (`ui/src/pod/overview/model.ts:17` `utcStamp`, "the mock's `stampOf`"), which is the tip the mock keeps. With that the page and the log say time the same way (relative, with UTC on hover; log clocks local with UTC on hover and an option).

## Decisions made here (for the lead to keep or reverse)

1. The tooltip rule above, and that a fast following list shows no tip on a row. 2. The prop is `timestamps` (the mock's option, whose Hidden value is P1b-4's); default local. 3. The clock's tip is the line's own timestamp. 4. A copy carries the clock the rows show (the mock copies local always). 5. Nothing changed for the rate.

- **Registry (`ui-next/shell/panel/logs/LogStreams.ts`, framework-free):** `open(target)` is the one entry point (a resource action today, a picker later). It calls the plugin's `open` once, makes the session in the panel (the panel opens on it), copies the feed's state to the session (`state.ts`: starting, streaming and paused are `starting`/`running`/`running`, ended, failed with the cause as one sentence in the view's own words), and closes the stream and releases its own listener when the tab closes, when the plugin unloads and when the window ends (`attach()` returns the stop). A `close()` that rejects or throws is logged. It never reads the feed and never pauses it. A wake-up for lines writes nothing to the panel (the status is compared by identity).
- **Body (`LogsBody.tsx`):** the kit's `LogView` over the registry's feed, in a box that fills the panel's slot (`LogsBody.module.css`: the slot lays a body out as an item of a row, which sized the view to its filter row, about half of the panel at 1440 px), wrapped in React's `Activity`: while the body is not on screen the view releases its effects (no listener on the stream, no frame, no timer: asserted with the fake clock, and in a real engine by the measurement above), keeps its lines and its place, and reads what the stream held when it comes back. The stream keeps holding its lines, bounded, meanwhile. A session with no stream (restored from the last run, or whose plugin was unloaded and came back) is no view: it is the panel's empty state saying `The earlier lines are gone` and `Open logs from the resource to follow them again.`

## Decisions made here (for the lead to keep or reverse)

1. **One `title` string, not head and tail.** The shell already cuts a session's title into a head that gives way and a tail that stays (SCT1, `splitTitle`); a plugin writes the tail last (`web-7d8c6f49b-f8q4m/app`). The descriptor has `title` and `scope`.
2. **A plugin that unloads loses its lines (PO1).** The panel hides its sessions while it is off and ends the running ones (`The plugin was unloaded.`); the registry closes their streams and drops them, and `close()` drops the stream's buffer; the hidden body is unmounted, so the view's lines go with it. The session comes back ended, with that cause and a body that says the lines are gone. Keeping them is the future buffer-policy row's (P1c); the alternative (keep the stream open for a history) leaks the backend session.
3. **The view is hidden with `Activity`, the panel's slot with `hidden`.** The panel slot cannot use `Activity` (a plugin's own body may need its effects while hidden); the host's Logs body can, because its stream is the registry's and not in its effects.
4. **`paused` is a fact, not a state** (the panel has four states): no pause control exists in the view or the panel yet (the kit's view only resumes), so today only a plugin or a test pauses a feed.
5. **Not built (not in the assignment, owner A20 and "keep it small"):** a pause control (see 4), a refusal of a session whose `open` throws that the user can see (the app has no notification surface; the console says it), and the Retry on a failed session (P1b1-12), which the lead allowed only if small, and it is not: the same tab must keep its earlier lines above a marker, but the kit's view starts empty for another feed ("another `feed` is another view"), so it needs a host-owned feed that outlives one stream (a wrapper that swaps the stream under the view and injects the marker line, keeping its status, its listeners and the order of `read`), a `retry` in the registry that calls the target's `open` again for a plugin that is still live (the registry must keep the target, and the panel model must allow a `failed` session to go back to `starting`), the card's second action, and tests for each: about as much again as the registry. The cap on hidden sessions' buffers is not built either: "Open" has its arithmetic.
6. **The log view gets a box that fills the slot (`LogsBody.module.css`).** The alternative, `flex: 1 1 auto` on the kit view's root, would change a kit part for what is the host's layout (the panel lays a body out as an item of a row).

## Open

- **No cap on the buffers of hidden sessions (the lead's ruling (c): not in this step).** Each session holds up to 100,000 lines in the view and up to 100,000 in the stream: 18.0 MB (view, 188 B a line, P1b-2 B4) + 23.8 MB (stream, about 240 B a line, P1b-1) = about 42 MB at both caps, so N sessions cost up to 42 x N MB: 10 sessions 420 MB, 25 sessions about 1 GB, 50 sessions 2.1 GB. A hidden session's view is fixed at what it had read when it was hidden and its stream goes on filling to its bound, so the worst case is a session that was hidden after it read a full view. Nothing bounds the number of sessions or their total; the policy (a total, an eviction of the oldest hidden, or a smaller stream bound for hidden ones) belongs to the buffer-policy row P1c.
- **Lines do not survive a plugin reload (the lead's withdrawn ruling (a), the owner accepts it).** A plugin that is off hides its sessions and ends the running ones, the host closes their streams, the views unmount, and what they had read is gone; the sessions return ended with a body that says so. Keeping the lines across a reload (a read-only history, or a `detach()` on the stream that keeps its buffer: backend catalog below) belongs to the buffer-policy row P1c.
- **The session tab shows the plugin's scope always; the mock shows it only to tell two open sessions with the same title apart.** The lead's ruling: do it here if it is a small change in the shell's tab labelling, else list it. Listed, because it is the panel model's identity rule: `core/panel/selectors.ts` `sessionItem` builds the tab's `scope` and its accessible name (`<title> · <scope>`) and groups twins by title AND scope (the ordinal `(2)`), while the mock's `labelParts` shows the scope only when another open session of the tool has the same title and another scope, and the ordinal only for sessions of one title and one scope. The step: group by title; show the scope only for a title that has two scopes among the visible sessions of the tool (`selectors.ts` with its tests; `SessionTabs.tsx` draws what the item says); the accessible name keeps the scope for those only; the card keeps its own facts. Tests that name a tab with a scope change: `PluginHost.logs.test.tsx` (`alpha/app · north`), `PluginHost.panel.test.tsx`, `Panel.test.tsx`, the seam check (`stream-alpha · north-yard`), and the panel probe's geometry for the scope part (its reference is the mock's, measured with twins). Until then P1b-3b's plugin should pass a `scope` only if it wants it on every tab.
- **The m4 summary's B3 wording** (`docs/ui-next/baseline/raw/2026-11-19-log-m4.json`) says "at every rate and after 100,000 lines" and "follow at every rate, scroll", while the run held the follow cell at 10,000 lines a second only: the analyzer's generic text (`bench/log/analyze.mjs`), to be scoped by the next run that touches it.
- **A view hidden for longer than its stream can hold loses lines, and the user is not told how many (found by the hidden-work probe's numbers, not a defect of this step).** By design the view reads nothing while it is hidden; the stream keeps the newest 100,000 lines (ten seconds at 10,000 a second) and drops the older ones, counted. What the user sees on coming back after about 13 s away from a 10,000 lines a second session (both engines, measured with the probe's feed and a throwaway script): the view holds the newest 100,000 lines (its whole buffer is replaced: the 65,000 it had are pushed out), follows the tail, and its strip says `100,000 lines · 35,050.0 lines/s · Following · 70,250 older lines dropped`. The stream's 30,200 dropped lines are in no number the user can see: the view appends one marker row (`30,200 lines dropped`) before the first held line, but the stream's bound equals the view's (100,000), so the marker is the oldest of 100,001 entries and is pushed out at once (scrolled to the top of the view there is no marker, in either engine), and the strip counts only the lines the view itself pushed out. The strip's rate also reads 35,000 to 38,000 lines/s for a few seconds (the burst the view took in is inside its 3 s window; the stream makes 10,000). Away for 5 s (50,000 lines held, none dropped; both engines, the same throwaway script) nothing is lost: the view keeps every line it had (scrolled to the top, its first row is still its first line) and appends the 50,000 that came meanwhile, with no marker and nothing about dropped lines in the strip, whose rate reads about 21,000 lines/s for a few seconds. A fix belongs to the kit's view or to the buffer-policy row P1c: count a marker's `dropped` in the strip when the marker leaves the buffer (or add the feed's `stats().dropped`), and decide what a hidden session's stream should hold.

## P1b-3c: the sessions of a host tool survive their plugin's unload (the exact spec)

Owner's word, through the lead (2026-11-19): keeping the lines is fine, and it is its own small step after this PR merges. This PR ships the mock's rule (PO1, ch16 S114a: a plugin that is off contributes nothing; its sessions are hidden and its running ones end; they come back ended when it does; the mock's `sessionsOf` in `shell-panel.js`: "A plugin that is off contributes nothing (PO1): its sessions are not listed, no tab, no row. They stay in the store, ended, and show again (still ended) when the plugin returns"). The step deviates from it for one case, and the deviation is to be recorded in `docs/ui-next/decisions.md` as a decision.

The rule: a session of a tool owned by the HOST (`Tool.owner === HOST`: today the Logs tool) that was ENDED BY ITS PLUGIN'S UNLOAD stays listed and mounted, ended, with the cause `The plugin was unloaded.`, its lines readable, until the user closes the tab. Everything else keeps PO1: a plugin's own tools and their sessions hide with it; a session restored from the last run (`RESTORED_CAUSE`) stays hidden until its plugin has loaded; a session that was already ended or failed when the plugin left was not ended by the unload and follows PO1 (hidden), unless the lead decides otherwise.

Files, all under `ui-next/shell`:

1. `core/panel/types.ts`: a session must say it was ended by the unload (a flag on `Session`, for example `held`: the cause is text, a flag is the honest key); `core/panel/persist.ts` never writes it (a restored session is not held).
2. `core/panel/tools.ts` `syncPlugins` (lines 100-108: the running sessions of a gone plugin end with the cause): set the flag when the session's tool is the host's; the plugin's return clears nothing (the session stays ended).
3. `core/panel/state.ts` `sessionVisible` (line 69: `tool visible && isLive(plugin)`) becomes `tool visible && (isLive(plugin) || held)`; `settle` (line 120) keeps what `sessionVisible` keeps, and the invariant `mounted and not visible` (line 167) follows from it; `selectors.ts` (`visibleSessions` is the one list the strip, the cards and the body read) needs no change while it calls `sessionVisible`.
4. `ui-next/shell/panel/logs/LogStreams.ts`: `reconcile` closes the stream of a plugin that left but keeps the entry and its feed object (the kit's view makes its model once per feed object, so the SAME object must stay the session's until the tab closes; the runtime's closed stream drops its buffer and publishes `ended`, and the view keeps the lines it had read), and releases the entry only when the session is gone. This is the registry of this PR's first iteration, with `end` and `release` split, `ended` on the entry and `reconcile` ending a plugin's streams without deleting them; its tests (red first, written then dropped by the ruling) were: after the unload the stream is closed once, its own listener is released, `feedOf` still gives the feed and `recordOf` the record, a later status the closed feed publishes writes nothing to the session, closing the tab releases the entry without a second close, a plugin's return keeps the old session and feed, the panel changing after the unload closes nothing twice, detaching the registry closes nothing a second time. `LogsBody.tsx` needs no change (`feedOf` gives the kept feed; `LOGS_GONE` is for a session with no entry: restored).
5. Tests that change or are added, red first: `core/panel/store.test.ts` and `fuzz.test.ts` (`checkInvariants`: `mounted and not visible` must hold for held sessions; the fuzz gets the operation: a plugin leaves and returns while held sessions exist), `core/panel/persist.test.ts` (never persisted as held), `panel/logs/LogStreams.test.ts` (the 'a plugin that goes away' tests of this PR, which say the entry is released at the unload, become the ones above), `panel/logs/LogsBody.test.tsx` (the lines stay after the unload: the invented feed publishes `ended` as it closes, as a real stream does), `plugins/host/PluginHost.logs.test.tsx` (the unload test: the tab stays, ended, the lines stay, closing the tab closes nothing twice), the panel probe (the shell's own fixture: a held session in the strip). The seam check cannot unload a plugin in its host today: no scenario there.
6. Decision to record: the deviation from PO1 for the host's tools, and why (the lines a person is reading are theirs; the stream is closed so the backend session is not leaked).

Not in the step: a history that outlives the tab, a buffer cap (P1c), lines across a restart of the app (the panel saves identity, never output).

## P1b-4: log session controls (the spec)

The mock's Logs tool has a vertical toolbar at the left of the body (Follow, Pause log updates, Wrap lines, Display options, Jump to time, Clear, Filter, Copy, Export, and `⋯` for the panel's options) and, in the filter row, `Since <duration>` (or `Time: …`), `Previous container` and `Container: …`. They are out of P1b-3a by the lead's ruling. Split:

- **What `LogView` already supports (cheap to surface):** follow (the view follows the tail and has `Jump to latest`; a toolbar toggle needs the view's follow state readable or controllable by the host), pause (the feed's `pause()` and `resume()`; the registry never pauses by design, a feed has one reader, so the control goes through the view or through a registry call the view is told of; today `paused` is only a fact of the card), clear (the view's model can drop its lines: a kit method), copy (the view has its `copy` event as plain text; a button for the selection or for all). Each is a tool action, so the panel needs a tool toolbar (a P1a frame addition: `PanelBindings` gets a `toolbarOf(session)`).
- **What needs new work:** `Since` / `Previous container` / `Container` are session OPTIONS that re-open the stream with other parameters (a contract change: `LogTargetV1.open(options)` with the options the plugin declares, and the registry's reopen that the Retry also needs, decision 5); wrap lines (the kit view has none: a kit change with its probe and the 100,000-line cost); display options (timestamps, line numbers, density: view options); jump to time (a search by time in the view's model); export (a save of the lines: a host service); the `⋯` menu of the panel's options.

## P1b-3b: the Kubernetes plugin opens a Pod's logs (the exact spec)

Repository `omniview-plugin-kubernetes`, base `feature/ui-next`. **Needs a host that has `ctx.panel` (this PR merged): the plugin says so in its minimum host version and tests `ctx.panel` at run time.**

1. In the Pod detail page's data hook, the commands get "Logs": one entry per container, and "All containers" when there is more than one (the containers come from the record the hook already holds: no Pod read, K8G-41). A command is `{ id, label, onSelect }`; with no `ctx.panel` it carries a `disabledReason` ("This version of Omniview cannot show logs here.").
2. `onSelect` calls `ctx.panel.logs.open({ id: 'pod/<namespace>/<name>/<container or *>', title: '<pod name>/<container>' (the container last, after a slash, so the tab keeps it whole), scope: '<connection name>', resource: { label: 'Pod <name>', kind: 'Pod', icon: <the Pod glyph>, route: { page: <the Pod detail page id>, connection, params: { namespace, name } } }, open: () => openLogStream({ plugin: ctx.plugin, connection, resourceKey: 'k8s:/pods', resourceId: <name>, resourceData: <the record>, reference, route: <the access route>, target: <container, or '' for all>, params: { filter_labels: 'container' } }) })`, with the same defaults as the old actions (tail 1,000, follow, previous off). `openLogStream` is `@omniviewdev/runtime/ui/v1` (P1b-1); its result is the feed, as it is.
3. The card: the host supplies kind, resource link, `Source`/`Sources` (the feed's `pod/container` ids), `Paused` and the state; a plugin's card completes it member by member (`completeLogCard`: a member the plugin leaves out stays the host's). Connection and Namespace are the plugin's: through `panel.sessions.logs.card`. A plugin's `facts` REPLACE the host's whole list, and `PanelSessionV1` carries no source list, so a plugin that gives `facts` must write the `Source(s)` row from what it knows (the container it opened); the simplest is to give no `facts` and let the card show the host's `Source(s)` and `Paused`.
4. What the plugin needs that is MISSING today: (a) nothing in the host contract beyond this PR; (b) the host Logs tool's Retry (decision 5) is the host's and is not built; (c) the real-cluster check of `.github/workflows/ui-next-pods.yml` (kind) must be extended: open a Pod's logs from its detail page and see the first line of a Pod that writes a known line, in the Logs tool, in the real host; close the tab and see the SDK's `ListSessions` return to 0 (the old UI never closed a session); (d) the logs probe (What remains, 1) should be green first, because the first real stream is the first time the Wails runtime, the registry and the view meet.
5. A later registry and picker (A20) need only: a list of the same `LogTargetV1` descriptors per plugin (a plugin-side function the host can call, with the connection), a cap policy for sessions of very large clusters, and the picker's own UX; the open function does not change.

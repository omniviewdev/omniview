## What the runtime's stream gives at open

Read in `packages/omniviewdev-runtime/src/logStream.ts` and `P1b-1.md` (sections 2 and 5, rows P1b1-05, -06, -11):

- **No marker.** A `LogLine` is `{ seq, source, timestamp, text, level, origin }`; `origin` is `current` for every line, `level` is never set, `LogBatch` is `{ lines, dropped, held }`. The session's events are seven (`SOURCE_ADDED`, `SOURCE_REMOVED`, `STREAM_ERROR`, `RECONNECTING`, `RECONNECTED`, `STREAM_ENDED`, `SESSION_READY`; SDK `stream.go:195-210`): none says "the tail is delivered". `SESSION_READY` is session-wide and means the first 20 sources have opened (`manager.go:1003-1032`), not that their tails were read. The request has `tailLines` (default 1,000) and `sinceSeconds`; the backend replays the tail and goes on, in the same stream, in the same batches.
- **Times are there.** `LogLine.timestamp` is the RFC 3339 text the backend wrote, '' when it has none (the zero time is mapped to '': P1b1-11). The Kubernetes plugin always asks for them (`handler.go:78`), and the host's lines carry a nanosecond UTC time (`P1b-3b.md`, "Wire").
- **The burst is observable.** On the kind cluster the 1,000-line tail arrived in two reads, 250 ms apart, and the first live line 0.5 s after the press.

So the options, in the assignment's order: a marker (none), the line's time against the open time less a small margin (available for the Kubernetes plugin, and for any plugin whose lines carry one), the first burst until the feed's first idle gap (the only thing a plugin with no times has). The model uses all three, the most exact first.

## The rule (`LogViewModel`, `packages/kit/src/log/logViewModel.ts`)

The rate is the feed's LIVE arrival rate (L1's: the feed's `received` over the wall clock, last 3 s). The model has an **opening** (from its construction until the replay is past) in which no series runs and the rate is 0. The opening ends at the first of:

1. **A line written after the view opened**: its time (`instantOf`, `log/time.ts`; written out, not `Date.parse`, which the engines do not read alike for nine fraction digits) is at or after the view's wall time at open less `OPEN_MARGIN_MS` (250 ms: the view opens a moment after the stream does, and two clocks are not one clock). The lines before it are the replay.
2. **For a line with no time** (or one that is not a time): the first read that finds lines at least `OPEN_GAP_MS` (150 ms: a burst's batches come within 50 ms, a source at 5 a second is 200 ms apart) after the previous read that found lines is the first live line; everything read before is the replay (the feed's burst).
3. **The window the rate is told over has passed** (3 s since the view opened): the replay does not keep the rate away longer, whatever the clocks say. This is what a backend whose clock is far behind this one, or a feed whose replay is slow, gets.

At the end the series starts: the baseline is the feed's count of lines given out so far (`received` less `held`, less the lines of this read after the first live one) at the clock now; the first live line is the instant zero and is not counted. The first rate is told once 250 ms have passed (L1's span rule). L1's hidden-view rule (`sample(true)` when the view comes back) and L3's paused and quiet rules are untouched; while the opening lasts `sample` does nothing, and after it everything is as it was. A view built over a feed that already holds lines (a panel shown late) reads them as the replay by their times, which is the same rule (they are the feed's past). **A view that detaches before the opening ends and comes back** (CodeRabbit): `sample(true)` notes the feed's count at each attachment during the opening (the lines accepted while it was away are the feed's past, L1), and the series that starts when the opening ends never starts below it; the replay accepted after the view came back stays excluded while the opening lasts. Without it the series started at 1 and the next tell read about 4,000 lines/s with no line arriving (seen red).

What it does not do (for the lead): **a backend clock AHEAD of the view's** by more than the margin makes the newest replay lines look live; the opening then ends early and the rate is inflated by (skew x rate) lines over the window, decaying within 3 s (measured in the skew table below). A clock BEHIND is the case 3 covers (rate 0 for at most 3 s, then right). A source whose replay arrives in two bursts more than 150 ms apart with no times has the second burst counted. Both are limits of an unmarked replay; the exact fix is a marker (Backend catalog).

Tests (`logViewModel.test.ts`, "the rate at open (L4)", 12 cases, the eighth from CodeRabbit's first review (a view that opened paused reads 0.0 when the opening expires with lines held) and four from its second: a view that detached before the opening ended and came back after 1,000 lines (stamped and not: 0.0, the stamped one seen red against the baseline of 1), the replay accepted after it came back still excluded, and the arrival order of the second source's late tail (pins the bound above); the harness gives its feed a wall clock and stamps lines with it): 5,000 lines replayed at open and then 5 a second for 15 s, the strip never reads above 10 and settles on 4 to 6, the lines stamped (the newest 1,000 ms and 200 ms before the open) and unstamped; 0.0 while the replay is read; a backend clock a minute behind; a source with a backlog that then goes quiet for 10 s and speaks; a source at 1,000 a second (told by 1.75 s). Seen red on the old model (7 of the first 7; the eighth red against the baseline `received - held` it replaced). Each rule was removed in turn and a test turned red for it (gap rule off: the unstamped test; times off: the busy source and the skewed ones; cap off: the skewed one), and nothing else did.

## What a backend clock that disagrees with the view's does (model, fake feed, `the rate at open (L4)` harness; measured, not reasoned)

A source that has written 5 lines a second for 1,000 s (5,000 lines replayed at open, the newest 1 s old on the backend's clock), then 5 a second; the backend's clock AHEAD of or BEHIND the view's by the amount in the first column; the strip's rate at every 250 ms tell (the first 12 tells are the first 3 s):

| backend clock | tells 1 to 14 |
|---|---|
| ahead 60 s | 1053.2 528.4 348.0 263.1 210.1 176.3 152.0 133.2 119.1 107.5 4.7 5.0 5.0 5.0 |
| ahead 5 s | 78.0 40.8 29.0 22.7 19.4 16.8 15.0 13.9 12.8 12.1 4.7 5.0 5.0 5.0 |
| ahead 1 s | 7.1 5.3 5.8 5.2 5.5 5.2 5.0 5.2 5.0 5.2 4.7 5.0 5.0 5.0 |
| behind 1 s | 0.0 0.0 0.0 3.4 5.0 4.6 4.3 4.8 4.6 4.9 4.7 5.0 4.8 5.0 |
| behind 5 s | 0.0 x 11, then 4.8 4.3 5.0 |
| behind 60 s | 0.0 x 11, then 4.8 4.3 5.0 |

So the claim of the rule section holds and is now measured: **behind** costs a rate of 0.0 for at most 3 s and is then right; **ahead** by 1 s is within the 2x bound (7.1), but by 5 s and 60 s the newest 25 and 300 replay lines look live and the strip reads 78 and 1,053 lines/s, decaying with the window (3 s). Only the backend's skew matters, and a Docker Desktop VM that slept can be seconds off.

**Proposal, prototyped and NOT included** (clock-free): replay = the lines delivered before the feed's first quiet gap (150 ms between two reads that found lines); the burst's newest line time is remembered; after the gap, the first line that has no time or is newer than that is the feed's own, and one that is not (a second source's late backlog) is still replay. Only differences between the feed's own times are read, so no clock agreement is needed. Same harness: every skew above, ahead and behind, gives the one series `0.0 2.9 4.6 4.3 4.9 4.6 4.5 4.8 4.6 4.9 4.7 5.0 5.0 5.0` (the rate is told at 0.5 s, from 0 to 60 s of skew either way), and all the L4, L1 and L3 tests stay green but one: a source that writes 1,000 lines a second has no quiet gap, so its replay is told apart only by the 3 s cap (`0 x 9, then 1115 1063 1044 1033 ...` against 900 to 1,100 by 1.75 s now). It also loses what the wall rule has when the clocks agree: a replay that reaches the view in pieces more than 150 ms apart has its second piece (newer than the first) counted live. It is about 20 lines. It trades a fast, exact rate for a busy source and for a slow replay when the clocks agree, against independence of the clocks; the lead decides.

## What the kubelet's log handling does to the first burst

- **Tail limit.** The request's `tailLines` (default 1,000) bounds each source's burst: a Pod with N containers replays up to N x 1,000 lines. The burst is therefore shorter than the file when the file is longer, never longer.
- **`sinceSeconds` / since time.** The burst is only the lines written in that span: their times are at most that old, still before the view's open (less 250 ms), so they are replay by rule 1; a span of 0.25 s or less puts all of them in the margin, which counts them live (at most that span's lines).
- **Rotated logs.** The kubelet serves the container's current log file; a file that just rotated holds fewer lines than the tail asked for and possibly none. The burst is then short or absent, the first line is a live line, the opening ends at once and the rate is 0.0 until the second live line. Lines in the rotated files are not replayed, and the plugin does not ask for the `previous` instance, so no older line comes after a newer one.
- **Several containers (what happens, exactly).** The opening is the VIEW's, not each source's: it ends at the first live line of ANY source. A second source's tail that is accepted BEFORE that line is replay and is excluded (every line is older than the open, and the rate has no series yet); a tail accepted AFTER it is NOT excluded: its lines enter the rate for the window they were accepted in. Measured (`logViewModel.test.ts`, the test of that arrival order: source A replays 5,000 lines and writes 5 a second for 2 s, then source B's 1,000-line tail, all older than the open, is accepted): the strip reads `483 426 379 343 319 319 319 319 320 319 319` and then `5` from the twelfth tell on (2.75 s), against a true 5; the first value is the tail over the 2 s the series had run, so a tail N lines long costs about N over the span for at most the 3 s window. Without times the same holds for a second burst more than 150 ms after the first. Both containers of a Pod start together, so the tails normally arrive in the first burst and this arrival order is the exception (a source that starts late, a container that restarts); it is **not fixed here and is closed by the SDK's per-source marker (L4-01, the step that adds it: S1)**, which says a source's tail is over and is per source. A per-source opening in this model is not small: the rate is the feed's counter (`received`: L1's and L3's rules for a hidden or paused feed need it to include lines not yet read), a line is classed when the view READS it, after it was counted, so excluding a late tail needs the counter corrected by lines of unknown acceptance time (negative rates for a view that came back) or a second counter; I did not do it.
- **Times.** The kubelet stamps each line with the node's clock when the runtime wrote it; two nodes (a Deployment's Pods) have two clocks, so skew is per source. The model reads each line against the view's wall clock, so a source on a node whose clock is ahead is the "ahead" row, one behind is the "behind" row.
- Not run: a cluster with rotated logs, `sinceSeconds`, or several nodes; this is from the plugin's request (`handler.go:74-108`) and the SDK's reader, read in P1b-1, and from the model's rules.

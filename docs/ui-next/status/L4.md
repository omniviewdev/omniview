## What the runtime's stream gives at open

Read in `packages/omniviewdev-runtime/src/logStream.ts` and `P1b-1.md` (sections 2 and 5, rows P1b1-05, -06, -11):

- **No marker.** A `LogLine` is `{ seq, source, timestamp, text, level, origin }`; `origin` is `current` for every line, `level` is never set, `LogBatch` is `{ lines, dropped, held }`. The session's events are seven (`SOURCE_ADDED`, `SOURCE_REMOVED`, `STREAM_ERROR`, `RECONNECTING`, `RECONNECTED`, `STREAM_ENDED`, `SESSION_READY`; SDK `stream.go:195-210`): none says "the tail is delivered". `SESSION_READY` is session-wide and means the first 20 sources have opened (`manager.go:1003-1032`), not that their tails were read. The request has `tailLines` (default 1,000) and `sinceSeconds`; the backend replays the tail and goes on, in the same stream, in the same batches.
- **Times are there.** `LogLine.timestamp` is the RFC 3339 text the backend wrote, '' when it has none (the zero time is mapped to '': P1b1-11). The Kubernetes plugin always asks for them (`handler.go:78`), and the host's lines carry a nanosecond UTC time (`P1b-3b.md`, "Wire").
- **The burst is observable.** On the kind cluster the 1,000-line tail arrived in two reads, 250 ms apart, and the first live line 0.5 s after the press.

So the options, in the assignment's order: a marker (none), the line's time against the open time less a small margin (available for the Kubernetes plugin, and for any plugin whose lines carry one), the first burst until the feed's first idle gap (the only thing a plugin with no times has). The model uses all three, the most exact first.

## The rule (`LogViewModel`, `packages/kit/src/log/logViewModel.ts`)

The rate is the feed's LIVE arrival rate (L1's: the feed's `received` over the wall clock, last 3 s). The model has an **opening** (from its construction until the replay is past) in which no series runs and the rate is 0. The opening ends at the first of:

1. **A line written after the view opened**: its time (`instantOf`, `log/time.ts`; written out, not `Date.parse`, which the engines do not read alike for nine fraction digits) is at or after the view's wall time at open less `OPEN_MARGIN_MS` (250 ms: the view opens a moment after the stream does, and two clocks are not one clock). The lines before it are the replay.
2. **For a line with no time** (or one that is not a time): the first read that finds lines at least `OPEN_GAP_MS` (150 ms: a burst's batches come within 50 ms, a source at 5 a second is 200 ms apart) after the previous read that found lines is the first live line; everything read before is the replay (the feed's burst).
3. **The window the rate is told over has passed** (3 s since the view opened): the replay does not keep the rate away longer, whatever the clocks say. This is what a backend whose clock is far behind this one, or a feed whose replay is slow, gets.

At the end the series starts: the baseline is the feed's count of lines given out so far (`received` less `held`, less the lines of this read after the first live one) at the clock now; the first live line is the instant zero and is not counted. The first rate is told once 250 ms have passed (L1's span rule). L1's hidden-view rule (`sample(true)` when the view comes back) and L3's paused and quiet rules are untouched; while the opening lasts `sample` does nothing, and after it everything is as it was. A view built over a feed that already holds lines (a panel shown late) reads them as the replay by their times, which is the same rule (they are the feed's past).

What it does not do (for the lead): **a backend clock AHEAD of the view's** by more than the margin makes the newest replay lines look live; the opening then ends early and the rate is inflated by (skew x rate) lines over the window, decaying within 3 s. A clock BEHIND is the case 3 covers (rate 0 for at most 3 s, then right). A source whose replay arrives in two bursts more than 150 ms apart with no times has the second burst counted. Both are limits of an unmarked replay; the exact fix is a marker (Backend catalog).

Tests (`logViewModel.test.ts`, "the rate at open (L4)", 7 cases; the harness gives its feed a wall clock and stamps lines with it): 5,000 lines replayed at open and then 5 a second for 15 s, the strip never reads above 10 and settles on 4 to 6, the lines stamped (the newest 1,000 ms and 200 ms before the open) and unstamped; 0.0 while the replay is read; a backend clock a minute behind; a source with a backlog that then goes quiet for 10 s and speaks; a source at 1,000 a second (told by 1.75 s). Seen red on the old model (7 of 7). Each rule was removed in turn and a test turned red for it (gap rule off: the unstamped test; times off: the busy source and the skewed ones; cap off: the skewed one), and nothing else did.

## The probe and its control (`ui-next/shell/probes/logs-open-probe.mjs`, fixture `logs-open-fixture.tsx`)

The host's own `LogSession` (the view, its model, the strip) over a feed that does what the real stream does at open: replays 5,000 lines, each stamped with the time it was written (the newest 1 s before the page opened), with nothing to mark them, in batches of 250 every 25 ms, then writes 5 a second stamped with the time it writes. The strip's text is read in the page every 250 ms for 12 s. Assertions: `premise.replay` (the whole replay was counted and the feed made 40 or more live lines: a feed that did nothing would pass the rest), `rate.max` (no sample above 10), `rate.told` (a rate of 3.5 to 6.5 within 5 s), `rate.settled` (from 6 s every sample 3.5 to 6.5). Seen **red on the old kit** (the `origin/main` build: `rate.max` in both engines) and green on this one (both engines).

Negative control `NEGATIVE_CONTROL=fresh`: the replay is stamped with the instant it is delivered, so no view could tell it from live lines; the strip then reads the burst (9,984 lines/s in its first sample, the demo's shape: 6,617, 4,966, ... 812, then 4.9 at 3.7 s). The probe runs the page twice in Chromium, without and with the fault, and the control is detected only when `rate.max` holds clean and fails perturbed and no other assertion but the rate's failed; **with `ASSERTION_OFF='^rate\.max '` the same fault passes and the control prints NOT DETECTED (exit 1)**, both seen. Both units are in the shard table (`scripts/ci/probe-shards.mjs`): 'Probe the rate on the Logs strip at open' in shard 8 and 'Probe that the rate at open probe sees a replay counted as live' in shard 9, 40 s each as ESTIMATES (locally 27 s and 30 s; shard 8 is 416 s and shard 9 421 s against the limit of 432; the limit is not raised); `node scripts/ci/probe-shards.mjs check` and its test pass.

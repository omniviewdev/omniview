Defect (recorded under "Open" in `status/P1b-3a.md`, found by the logs probe): lines the STREAM dropped while the view was not reading (a hidden panel at 10,000 lines a second for more than about 10 s) were lost without the user being told. The view did append a marker row for them, but the stream's bound equals the view's (100,000), so the marker was the oldest entry of a full buffer and went in the frame that appended it; the strip counted only the view's own evictions; and the rate read about 35,000 lines/s for a few seconds after returning because the catch-up burst was counted as live.

## What was built

(a) **One number, in the mock's words.** The mock's strip says `N older lines dropped` (`shell-panel.js` `statText`, S120) and puts the sentence "N older lines dropped. The viewer keeps the latest LOG_CAP" in a marker row; there is one sentence and no breakdown. So the strip says ONE total, `N older lines dropped`, which is the lines the view evicted plus the lines the feed dropped before the view read them (`StatsSnapshot.dropped`; `unread` is the feed's part, `capacity` the view's bound). What it is made of is the strip's tooltip (`data-ov-tip`) and its accessible description (`aria-description`), `droppedNote` in `log/statText.ts`: `30,200 lines never reached the view: the feed held too many while the view was not reading. 71,300 older lines were pushed out of the view, which keeps the latest 100,000.` No note while nothing was dropped.

(b) **A gap stays visible where it occurred.** `LineView` (log/lineView.ts) keeps a marker that reaches the head of the buffer: the view holds what it stood for (`headDropped`, the sum of the markers that reached the head) and shows ONE row before the oldest entry the buffer holds, the "lead", for as long as the view holds any line. It is one row beyond the buffer's own bound (the buffer is untouched), numbered one before the buffer's first ordinal (an ordinal no entry holds any more, so the ordinals of the view stay in a row), made when asked for and cached while its ordinal and count stay. `at`, `ordinalAt`, `lowerBound` and `length` tell of it; the model, the search and the list read the view only through those. `pushed` and `shrunk` return how many rows left the head of the list: a marker that becomes the lead is a row that did not leave (it is where it was), so a reader scrolled back is not moved. A marker with lines before it stays where it is in the buffer, as before. When the gap is older than everything held, the strip's count still carries it (the model counts `batch.dropped` itself, not what the rows say).
  - Consequence to know: a marker that has reached the head never leaves while the view holds a line, so a view that ever dropped lines to the feed shows `-- N lines dropped --` as its first row for good; N is the sum of the markers that reached the head, not of every line the view evicted since (the strip says that). The mock's drop marker is likewise the first row of its list while the window shows the oldest kept line.

(c) **The rate is the feed's.** `rate` is the feed's own counter of lines accepted (`LogFeedStats.received`) over the wall clock, sampled at each tell of the strip (about four a second) and over the last three seconds, NOT rows appended per frame: a catch-up of lines the feed held is not a lines-a-second the feed makes. A view that starts or comes back starts the series from the feed's count at that moment (the lines held meanwhile are the feed's past); a feed that was quiet for over the window and speaks starts it from its last count and from now, so the lines that woke it are counted and the quiet is not. Until a span of 250 ms exists the strip keeps the rate it last told. The strip stops asking for frames when the rate is 0.

(d) **The first lines are told at once.** The first frame that reads a line sets the strip's clock due (`toldLines`), so it no longer reads `0 lines` for up to 250 ms after the first lines draw; later updates stay at four a second.

Probe (`ui-next/shell/probes/logs-probe.mjs`, the one probe file this step may edit): after the view caught up with the 13 s hidden at 10,000 lines a second, the sources go quiet (`__fixtureLogs.stopSources()`), the view reads the rest, and then **emitted = shown + told-dropped** exactly (the sources' count against the strip's `N lines` and `M older lines dropped`), the note's `N lines never reached the view` is the stream's own dropped count, the strip says at least the lines the stream dropped on the way back, and the strip's rate while the view catches up and for 3.5 s after is at most 1.5 times the feed's 10,000 (and 0.5 to 1.5 times at the end). Its comments and header say so.

## Method (as written in `status/P1b-2.md`)

Every trial is a fresh browser context and page. One round runs every config once, the order rotating from round to round, so machine drift hits every config alike: 10 rounds, so 10 paired trials. A follow trial: 1 s of idle cadence, the buffer filled to its cap (100,000 lines), 1 s, then the source at its rate for 3 s unmeasured and 10 s measured, with the list following the end; the control is the same page with a scroller of plain `div` rows over the same window maths (`windowOf`), the same bound and the same loop shape, no React, no ANSI parse, no level detection, no search, no selection. (The control page still LOADS React's module: `main.tsx` imports `Profiler` statically, for the profiling build, before it chooses a variant; the control never renders with it, so its frame times are what they are, but "no React on the page" is about its rendering, not its bytes: CodeRabbit's comment on `main.tsx`, open.) A scroll trial: the full buffer, 30 pages down by paced wheel input (60 events a second, 1,200 px/s, sent from Node), then the old viewer's own workload, 30 one-page jumps 250 ms apart. A search trial: a literal, a regex, no match, and the literal typed at 80 ms a key, each timed from the input event to the commit that puts the count the page itself computed (the view's rule over the same seeded lines) in the find lane. The memory trial (Chromium): the heap after two forced collections with nothing held, with 100,000 lines held, and after another 1,000,000 lines at 50,000 a second.

## What the bench found, before it ran in full

The harness found two defects in the view before the matrix, in its first shakedown (both fixed with the test written first, seen red; `LogView.test.tsx`, `logViewModel.test.ts`):

1. **At the cap the list froze.** Once the buffer was full the list stopped at one line while the strip went on counting: the rows were read from the model by index during render, the React Compiler cached the render on the window (a start and an end), and at the cap the window stops changing (T-10). The rows now come in the snapshot.
2. **A second commit in some frames, 61 a second at 10,000 lines a second.** The strip's timer committed on its own, and its store answered differently between React's two reads around a commit (the rate is a window over the clock), so React committed again to repair it (MNR-L2). The strip is now told in the frame and its read is a pure read.

The matrix then found the two misses above (B5's frame, B4's flatness). The bench itself was corrected twice before it ran in full: the overscan patch of the page build (the kit's `dist` went over Babel's 500 KB and its output lost the spaces the pattern expected), and the "newest line shown" check of the plain-DOM control (which draws a line's colour escapes as they are).


## What this does not measure

Native WKWebView, Windows and Linux engines, a device pixel ratio other than 1, real fonts (the page uses the theme's), hardware rendering (both engines run headless under software rendering), a wrapped list (not built), the panel's host around the view (P1b-3), the view over a real `openLogStream` (P1b-3), WebKit's heap (a page's heap cannot be read from outside; Chromium's is).

## Results of the third run (m3): the follow, scroll and exact-count cells on the final code

m1 measured these cells on `a8364f68`. The view changed after it in ways that bear on exactly them: the feed's ring (B4's fix: the kit and the control share the feed, so the comparison between them stood, but the absolute figures moved), the pause of a feed that is over, the feed's bounded catch-up and, for the follow cells above all, `aria-rowcount` and `aria-rowindex`, which became the list's own positions. A row's position is a prop of the memoised `LogRow`, so at the cap every frame that evicts moves the position of every mounted row that is kept: the row renders again and writes one attribute. m1 could not have contained that cost, and it was the one named as unmeasured. m3 measures the cells again on the final code, with the rule unchanged (the statistic that judges each budget is in the analyzer's header, written before m1): B1 follow (with the plain-DOM control), B2 commits, B3 rows, B6 scroll, B8 listeners, B9 writes at the root and the commit counter's cross-check, in WebKit and Chromium, 10 interleaved rounds each. Not measured at m3, so as measured before the review run's changes: B4 (m2), B5 (m2) and B7 (m1).

**Result: every budget these cells carry passes in both engines, and no fix was needed.** The cell asked for, follow at 10,000 lines a second with the buffer at the 100,000 cap, passes: Chromium rAF p95 16.7 ms against 16.7; WebKit kit 17.5 ms [17.2-18.0] against the control's 17.7 ms [17.3-17.9], limit 19.4 ms, per-round ratio 1.006 [0.983-1.006].

**What "at the cap" is here.** The follow workload has always filled the buffer to its cap before the source starts (`run.mjs`, `followTrial`: `fill(100_000)`, then `drained()`; every trial's record says `received: 100000` and a `scrollHeight` of 2,400,025 px, which is 100,000 rows of 24 px and the 25 px header, before the first line of the stream), and then runs the source at its rate. At 10,000 lines a second every 16.7 ms frame appends about 167 lines to a full buffer and the ring evicts the same number (the cells' `emitted/s` is 10,042). So the cell at 10,000 lines a second is the B1 cell at 10,000, measured now on the code that moves the positions. At that rate none of the 32 mounted rows is kept from one frame to the next (167 new lines against 32 rows: arithmetic from the rate, not counted in a trial), so the position's move costs no more than a new row does there. The rate at which the move costs the most is a stream so slow that every mounted row is kept and moves a place or two a frame: 100 lines a second (1.7 lines a frame, so about 30 of the 32 rows are kept and their positions move: the same arithmetic). That is the one cell m1 did not have and m3 adds (the kit and its control; reported, because the rule gives no budget at that rate). The kit's frame work there is its lowest: p95 0.90 ms in Chromium against the control's 0.63, and 1.55 ms in WebKit against 1.05, while at 1,000, 10,000 and 50,000 lines a second it is 1.22, 1.49 and 1.53 ms (Chromium) and 2.52, 3.20 and 3.36 ms (WebKit), as at m1 (1.30, 1.52 and 1.69 ms; 2.48, 3.53 and 3.35 ms). The move of the positions therefore shows no cost above the noise, and the stable offset the rule names for a miss (accessible positions that stay correct without writing every mounted row each frame) was not built: there was no miss to fix.
